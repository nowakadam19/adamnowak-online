A mid-size retailer launched a points programme with a solid business case. Active member rate projected at 38%. Frequency uplift assumed at 14%. The P&L looked clean.

Twelve months in, active rate landed at 22%. Frequency uplift was 6%.

The programme wasn't a failure. It was profitable. But the business case was built around 38% — and when 22% arrived, there was no playbook for it.

## The problem with scenarios

Most loyalty business cases include three scenarios: base, optimistic, conservative. They exist to show the CFO you've thought about risk.

They rarely change the decision. Everyone knows which one the team believes in.

The conservative scenario is usually "base minus 20%." It's not built from a real hypothesis about what could go wrong. It's base, discounted for comfort.

This is not sensitivity analysis. It's decoration.

## What sensitivity analysis actually is

The useful question is not "what if things go slightly worse?"

It's: **at what point does this programme stop making financial sense — and what would we do then?**

That's a different exercise. You're not adjusting assumptions to show range. You're stress-testing the model to find the break point.

Where does active rate have to land for the programme to lose money? What happens if basket uplift doesn't materialise at all — and frequency carries everything? What if the referral mechanic overperforms by 3x?

These aren't edge cases. They're the map you navigate with when reality diverges from the plan — and it always does.

## The surprises go both ways

The retailer above had a problem nobody modelled: their referral mechanic outperformed by a factor of four. New member acquisition was running hot. The programme was absorbing acquisition costs that hadn't been budgeted.

It was a good problem. But it was still a problem — because nobody had built the operational response in advance.

Benefits appear from unexpected places. Costs too. The teams that handle this well are not the ones with the most accurate forecast. They're the ones who ran enough scenarios to recognise the pattern when it arrived.

The [Loyalty Programme ROI Calculator](/tools/loyalty-roi-calculator) is built for this kind of thinking.

Don't run it once with your base assumptions. Run it three times with intent:

**Stress test.** Drop active rate to the floor of what's realistic. Cut every uplift assumption in half. What's the required lift now? Is it still defensible?

**Upside scenario.** What if referral overperforms? What if retention improvement comes in stronger than expected because you nailed the emotional mechanic? Where does that take your revenue multiple?

**Sensitivity check.** Move one variable at a time. Which input has the most impact on your ROI? That's where your operational attention belongs — not spread equally across everything.

The goal is not a better forecast. It's knowing what you'll do when the forecast is wrong.
