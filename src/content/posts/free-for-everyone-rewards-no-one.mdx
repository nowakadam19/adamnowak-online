title: "Free for everyone rewards no one"
date: "2026-09-22"

On World Car-Free Day the trains ran free, and the only passengers who noticed were the ones who had already paid.

**Why it matters:** A benefit handed to everyone with no condition attached changes nobody's behaviour. You pay for what would have happened anyway, and the cost lands on the customers you most wanted to keep.

**The evidence:** I got on a train this morning, opened the app and the payment failed. The passenger next to me told me not to bother. Car-Free Day, everything runs free today. He was in the seat he takes every morning, and so was everyone else in the carriage. Nobody on that train had left a car at home to be there. The one person who might have was in his car.

The operator gave away a day of fares. Here the operator is subsidised, so the money was the taxpayer's, which makes it easier to spend and harder to count. Nobody will measure whether a single driver switched, because nobody will be asked to. What the day produced is a line for a report. We took part.

The people with monthly passes paid the usual price for a day everyone else got free. That is the part that carries over to loyalty.

Programmes run the same event every day of the year. A point on every purchase is the clean case. A member earns one euro on a fifty-euro basket, does not notice, and changes nothing about the next basket, and the finance team meets the total at year end as a cost of doing business. The same goes for the sign-up discount that every new customer receives, and the birthday voucher that goes out whether or not the member has bought anything since the last one. None of it asks the customer for a single decision.

The defence never changes. It builds awareness, it builds goodwill. Both are claims about a number nobody has been asked to produce. A benefit that cannot be measured cannot fail, which is why it survives the budget rounds that kill things that can.

**What to do Monday:** Take every benefit in the programme and put two questions next to it.

- **What does the customer have to do to get it?** If the answer is nothing, it is a discount, and the pricing budget should be paying for it.
- **Would they have done it anyway?** If the answer is yes, the benefit is paying for behaviour you already had.
- **Move the money.** Whatever fails both questions goes to a benefit with a condition, the second visit or the referral or the first order in a new channel, or to the people with monthly passes, who have been paying for everyone else's free day.

**The bottom line:** Free for everyone is the easiest benefit to approve and the hardest to justify. Attach a behaviour to it, or hand it to the people who already pay you, and it starts buying something.
