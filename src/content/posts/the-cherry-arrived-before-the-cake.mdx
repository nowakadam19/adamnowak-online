---
title: "The cherry arrived before the cake"
date: "2026-09-21"
excerpt: "The loyalty industry has spent a decade designing emotional engagement. It never checked whether the programme underneath it works."
tags: ["emotional engagement", "loyalty", "evidence"]
---

Everyone in loyalty is designing the cherry and nobody has checked whether there is a cake.

**Why it matters:** Emotional engagement has been the industry's favourite phrase for ten years, and it now leads most answers to any request for proposals. It costs real money, it cannot be planned, and the programmes it is meant to crown are often still missing their base layer.

**The evidence:** The pitch usually opens with the line that a programme which needs explaining has already lost, followed by a list of emotional attachments with no link to anything a customer does or spends. If your brand looks like the others, add emotion. Every one of these claims comes with the same two witnesses, Apple and Patagonia, who have been giving the same testimony for a decade. Both businesses run on products, pricing and distribution. None of that sits in a loyalty team's budget.

Then comes the number. Fully connected customers are worth 52% more than merely satisfied ones. It comes from a 2015 Harvard Business Review piece written by people who ran the consultancy that measured the connection and sold the method. One proprietary dataset, cited in more decks than I can count, and I have yet to see it reproduced inside a loyalty programme by anyone who was not selling it. That is a bibliography with one entry.

Emotion behaves like a viral post. Nobody knows in advance which one will land, and the budget that produced one hit paid for twenty misses. Plot emotion against sales and the line stays flat for most of its length. Plot emotion against cost and it climbs.

Meanwhile, the cake. Points post on time. The person at the counter can see your tier without asking, and the agent on the phone has your history open before you finish the sentence. There is no emotion in any of it, and it is where the thing the decks call engagement actually comes from, because customers engage with what works and drift away from what doesn't. Members leave when their points go missing. None of them has ever left over a missing moment.

**What to do Monday:** Three checks, none of them expensive.

- **Count the pages.** Take the last programme review and count the pages on emotion, then the pages on defects such as posting delays, tier recognition failures, login errors and complaint resolution time. If the first number is bigger, the review was written for the board.
- **Ask for the other version of the number.** Any budget line with "emotional" in it should come with a measurement produced by someone other than the vendor selling the line.
- **Run the boring test.** If a member were treated correctly for a full year, every point posted and every status honoured, without a single moment designed to move them, would they leave? If the honest answer is no, the cake is the programme and the cherry can wait.

**The bottom line:** The industry has spent a decade on cherry design. Bake the cake first. Most customers will settle for one that arrives.
