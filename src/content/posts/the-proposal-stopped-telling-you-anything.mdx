A document that costs nothing to produce tells you nothing about whoever produced it.

**Why it matters:** The pitch process never measured how good an agency was. It measured whether they could build a document, and that worked only because building it was expensive. Weeks of work. Senior people pulled off live accounts. A decision, taken early, about which pitches were worth entering at all. The cost was the information. Whoever spent the most was telling you they wanted it most and could afford to.

Now the brief is drafted with a model, the response is drafted with a model, and the evaluation matrix is filled in with a model. That is ordinary enough. It also removed the last piece of friction that made the exercise partly informative.

Both sides now optimise against the same rubric using the same tool, so the scores converge. Everyone scores high and nobody separates. Once the qualitative section stops sorting anyone, the decision falls to the only criterion left with real variance, which is price. A process built to find the best supplier now reliably finds the cheapest one and reports it as an objective result.

The decision itself does not disappear. It moves. It ends up in corridor conversations, in who performed well in the room, in who you already know. The formal process stays on as theatre while the real choice happens somewhere procurement cannot see it. That is worse governance than running no process at all.

Pitching was already broken before any of this. It was just slower, and slowness was doing more work than anyone admitted.

**The evidence:** Go back to your last pitch and check three things.

- **Score spread.** If the qualitative scores across shortlisted agencies sit within a few points of each other, that section did no work.
- **Named people.** Count how many of the people named in the winning document have since touched the account.
- **Deciding moment.** Ask the panel when they made up their minds, and whether it was before or after reading the submissions.

**What to do Monday:** Stop buying evidence of writing and start buying evidence of doing. Replace the final presentation round with a paid working session on a real problem you currently have. Insist on meeting the named team instead of reading their biographies. Treat a small paid pilot as the last stage rather than a deck.

**The bottom line:** Cost was doing the sorting all along. Put cost back somewhere a model cannot carry it for either side, and the process starts telling you things again.
